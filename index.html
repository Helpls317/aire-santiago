🛡️ Protecting Yourself from Pollution

Steps you can take when air quality is poor — especially on unhealthy or very unhealthy days.

😷

Wear a mask

An N95 or KN95 mask filters out fine particles (PM2.5) effectively. Standard surgical masks offer some protection but let more particles through. Fit matters — press the nose wire snug.

From: Moderate
🏠

Stay indoors

Keep windows and doors closed on high-pollution days. Air conditioning on "recirculate" mode (not fresh-air intake) keeps outdoor particles out. A HEPA air purifier indoors makes a big difference.

From: Unhealthy
🚫🏃

Avoid strenuous exercise outside

Physical activity makes you breathe faster and deeper, pulling more pollutants into your lungs. Move workouts indoors or reschedule to early morning when Santiago's air is typically cleaner.

From: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups
🚗

Check vehicle restrictions

Santiago enforces restricción vehicular (licence plate restrictions) on bad air days, and can declare a pre-emergencia or emergencia ambiental. Check UOCT or the SINCA portal for the day's status.

From: Moderate
🪵

Don't burn wood or rubbish

Wood-burning stoves, bonfires, and burning rubbish are major contributors to Santiago's winter smog. On restricted days, using a wood stove is illegal. Switch to electric or gas heating if possible.

From: Unhealthy
👶🧓

Extra care for vulnerable groups

Children, elderly people, pregnant women, and anyone with asthma, heart disease, or lung conditions are most at risk. Keep them indoors earlier, avoid rush-hour streets, and consult a doctor if symptoms flare up.

From: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups
🚌

Use public transport or cycle

Fewer cars on the road means less NOx and particulate matter. On good air days, cycling on CicloVías is a great option — just check the AQI first and pick a route away from heavy traffic.

Always helpful
💧

Stay hydrated

Drinking plenty of water helps your respiratory system flush out irritants. Combined with dry Santiago air (especially in summer), dehydration can make pollution's effects on the throat and lungs feel worse.
